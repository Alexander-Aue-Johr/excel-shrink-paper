\subsection{Workbook bloat and cleanup tools}
Microsoft Check Performance removes cells associated with unnecessary formatting
and other stored information from a loaded workbook~\cite{checkperformance}.
Its documentation warns that optimization can remove pixel-art cells. Inquire's
Clean Excess Cell Formatting command removes excess cells beyond the last
nonblank cell and can change conditional-formatting coverage~\cite{inquirecleanup}.
O'Beirne's published analysis of Excel 2013 Inquire discusses this command and
criticizes its lack of information about planned and completed
removals~\cite{obeirne2013inquire}. This historical analysis establishes published
attention to cleanup and its transparency. Current product behavior requires
version-specific evidence. These documented policies motivate comparing both
reduction and preservation outcomes under explicit contracts.

Package-level cleanup also predates SheetShrink. XLStylesTool's developer
describes an Open-XML-based cleaner for excessive and corrupted styles that
operates independently of Excel's object model~\cite{xlstylestool}.
NXPowerLite Desktop 10 lists excess XLSX cell-format cleanup among its
features~\cite{nxpowerlite10}. Its engine description discusses formatted blank
cells as a source of large worksheet parts~\cite{nxengine}. These are relevant
comparison subjects. Their available descriptions do not establish the
guarded removal rules, compact row-font witnesses or native preservation
outcomes examined here. Those properties require version-specific evidence.
SheetShrink's positioning therefore includes its effect-aware rules, retained
representatives and measured costs alongside its execution before native loading.
Its removal decisions use persisted OOXML properties and workbook context.
The cleanup phase has no native rendering observations or measured automatic
row heights available to guide deletion or reconstruct layout. Small retained
font and inheritance witnesses must preserve these effects when Excel later
opens the result. Native validation supplies subsequent experimental evidence
for the decisions described in the method.

\subsection{Spreadsheet scalability and system optimization}
Mack et al.'s CHI Extended Abstracts case study characterizes scalability
problems reported in spreadsheet forums~\cite{mack2018scalability}. Rahman et al.
benchmark Excel, LibreOffice Calc and Google Sheets on spreadsheet computations
and examine indexing, data layout, incremental evaluation and shared
computation~\cite{rahman2020benchmarking}. These studies motivate performance
work on spreadsheets. Their workloads assess system responsiveness and
computation, while SheetShrink measures removable persisted representation and
the cost of preparing workbooks for subsequent use.

DataSpread's storage engine integrates spreadsheet presentation with database
storage and positional access~\cite{bendre2018pdm}. Anti-Freeze schedules
asynchronous formula computation to retain interactivity during
updates~\cite{bendre2019antifreeze}. Hillview distributes exploration of large
datasets and uses compact visualization sketches~\cite{budiu2019hillview}.
TACO exploits regular formula-reference patterns to compact dependency graphs
and query them directly~\cite{tang2023taco}. These systems optimize storage,
computation or exploration within a processing system. SheetShrink transforms
an existing XLSX package into another editable workbook under a stated
preservation contract. These optimization layers can be used together.

\subsection{Spreadsheet loading and specialized parsing}
SheetReader studies specialized spreadsheet ingestion and the coupling of
parsing, decompression and parallel execution~\cite{sheetreader}. It motivates
evaluating loading cost and memory. Its output consists of data representations
for an analytical consumer. SheetShrink produces an editable workbook whose
required layout and dependencies must survive package rewriting. A blank cell
that a tabular reader can omit may still influence inherited formatting or
automatic layout in Excel. Our evaluation therefore measures cleanup together
with subsequent use of the output and separately observes native preservation.
The two systems address complementary sources of ingestion cost, namely the
reader's processing strategy and the representation supplied to it.

SIMD-aware parsing provides a methodological connection to simdjson.
Langdale and Lemire use SIMD operations for high-throughput JSON
parsing~\cite{simdjson}. SheetShrink applies SIMD-assisted library searches to
XML byte buffers, combined with application-specific state machines and
preservation rules. The shared principle is accelerating byte classification
and search while retaining the format's recognition obligations. The concrete
search implementation in SheetShrink is supplied by \texttt{memchr}, whose
runtime dispatch and reusable searchers are described in our method~\cite{memchr281}.

\subsection{Spreadsheet refactoring and validation}
Hermans et al. detect formula smells and suggest refactorings to improve
spreadsheet maintainability~\cite{formulasmells}. Their work connects structural
changes to behavioral obligations. SheetShrink's removal rules additionally
account for formatting inheritance and native layout effects of stored cells.

Spreadsheet slicing integrates dependency reasoning with testing and
debugging~\cite{slicing}. Dependencies also constrain removal when retained
formulas or graphical objects refer to candidate content. Workbook comparison
tools provide observations about cell and format differences~\cite{spreadsheetcompare}.
Our native validation protocol uses DeltaSieve's region selection and capture
workflow~\cite{deltasievecompanion}, with independent references and mutation
controls to evaluate selected coverage. These validation mechanisms provide
evidence about cleanup outcomes after the package transformation.
