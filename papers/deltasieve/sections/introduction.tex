A spreadsheet transformation can preserve cell values and still change the
visible document. Removing an empty formatted cell can expose a background or
alter automatic row height. Changing a referenced input can affect a distant
conditional-formatting region or chart. Checking only edited cell payloads
therefore leaves important transformation effects unobserved.

Native rendering supplies an operational way to observe these effects in the
spreadsheet application that users employ. Exhaustive rendering, however, can
be expensive for large sheets and corpora. Stored worksheet extents may also
include empty or uniformly formatted tails. Dividing these extents by a fixed
cell grid can create many images despite very little finite visible content.
The challenge is to select useful rendering work while retaining explicit
uncertainty about unsupported features and incomplete comparisons.

Figure~\ref{fig:validation-overview} summarizes the selection and evidence workflow.
DeltaSieve combines package-level change analysis with native visual comparison.
It follows effective style identities across table renumbering, considers
layout and dependency effects, and records the reasons for its capture choices.
Supported conditional-formatting and chart dependencies can establish that an
unchanged input cannot have changed the associated presentation under the
supported model. Changed or unresolved dependencies remain capture candidates.
The native stage measures geometry, renders bounded image batches, and compares
retained evidence under a recorded state-pair policy.

The scientific question concerns the detection and cost of this selection.
A dependency-aware planner is useful only if its reduced work retains the
relevant effects in the evaluated domain. We therefore separate selector
coverage, pixel observation and defect attribution. Independent full-reference
samples and mutations test detection. Repeated same-input observations test
rendering variability. A changed image remains an observation whose cause may
require further evidence.

Existing spreadsheet dependency slicing motivates careful treatment of indirect
effects~\cite{slicing}. Existing workbook comparison tools also provide reports
of structural and cell-level changes~\cite{spreadsheetcompare}. DeltaSieve's
evaluation must establish the additional contribution of its native-rendering
selection and evidence workflow. An interactive Observatory and portable report
make those observations inspectable. They support the method's evaluation without
replacing its independent detection tests.

DeltaSieve grew out of the need to investigate visual regressions in SheetShrink,
a workbook cleanup system described in a companion manuscript~\cite{sheetshrinkcompanion}.
That manuscript studies cleanup rules and preservation structures. Here we
study the selection, capture and evidence needed to evaluate rendered changes
across workbook transformations, including alternative cleanup tools. \ifdefined\withoutreferences
